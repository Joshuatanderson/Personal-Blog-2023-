Before this week, my impression of New York was negative. It was a beehive of people, with thoughts clouded by the world, sacrificing their empathy, and souls, on the altar of success, with most of them being left behind along the way.

But, as they say, the same man never crosses the same river twice. The last time I was in NYC, I held a lot of cult-like beliefs. Now, I have a radically different belief system (although in the total value manifold for human values, it may actually be similar, and I'd be curious about trying to quantify this somehow).

The lack of othering New Yorkers, along with having a different value set, has made me notice a lot of new things about this city.

New Yorkers are actually, as a mentor of mine remarked as he gave me a ride to the airport, not nice. But they are kind, and that's more important. They won't typically engage with you unless spoken to, or unless they have a reason to interact with you. There's a sort of silent agreement across the millions of people - we'll go insane if we all talk to each other. With tens of millions of people around, one has to sieze solitude in moments on the subway, surrounded by 50 people.

There is an acknowledgement - expressed by an unspoken, wordless consent, that time is precious, and it flees by more visibly in the city that never sleeps than anywhere else. People party until the dawn, because they're acutely aware that one day, they'll never wake again.

Unlike the south, it's a city of realists, unbothered by a hollow promise of an afterlife, but unaided by the comfort of sweet lies.

New York drinks whisky like water, fucks like its her last night alive, and more than anything else, lives.

And that's why I love it.
